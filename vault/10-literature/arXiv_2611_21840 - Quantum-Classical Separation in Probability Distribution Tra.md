# Quantum-Classical Separation in Probability Distribution Transport via Nonlocal Games

## Abstract

> In many-body physics and generative artificial intelligence, locality provides a natural notion
> of phase equivalence: two quantum states or classical probability distributions are regarded as
> in the same phase when local dynamics can reversibly transform one into the other. This raises a
> fundamental question: can local quantum dynamics transport classical probability distributions
> in ways that local classical dynamics cannot? We establish a quantum-classical separation in
> distribution transport: two classical distributions can be connected exactly and reversibly by a
> constant-depth local quantum circuit, whereas classical stochastic transport requires a
> substantially larger spatial range. We demonstrate this separation explicitly on a one-
> dimensional chain and develop a general construction principle on graphs, based on nonlocal
> games. Moreover, the source distribution can itself be generated by a simple local classical
> process, establishing a quantum advantage in distribution generation as well. Our results reveal
> a fundamental distinction between classical and quantum local reversibility, even when both the
> initial and final states are entirely classical.
