# Machine learning quantum data to accelerate quantum Monte Carlo

## Abstract

> Simulating strongly correlated electrons in molecules and materials remains a central goal for
> quantum computing. A promising strategy uses quantum processors to prepare trial wavefunctions
> that guide classical quantum Monte Carlo (QMC). However, the enormous computational cost of
> repeatedly processing quantum measurements has severely limited the practical scalability of
> this approach. Here, we overcome this bottleneck by compressing quantum measurement data into
> machine-learning models that efficiently provide the wavefunction overlaps needed by QMC. This
> accelerates quantum-classical QMC by orders of magnitude, enabling chemically accurate
> simulations on up to 40 qubits, more than doubling the scale of previous hardware demonstrations
> with comparable accuracy. We demonstrate our approach on hydrogen chains and strongly correlated
> iron-sulfur clusters using measurements from Google's Willow processor. By enabling efficient
> reuse of quantum-state information on the classical coprocessor, our framework addresses a
> fundamental bottleneck in hybrid quantum algorithms that will persist even into the fault-
> tolerant era.
