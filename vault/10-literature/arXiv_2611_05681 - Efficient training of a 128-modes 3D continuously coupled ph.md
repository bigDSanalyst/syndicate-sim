# Efficient training of a 128-modes 3D continuously coupled photonic chip via limited quantum resources

## Abstract

> Integrated photonics provides a promising route towards the development of large-scale, stable
> and low-loss quantum hardware. Existing implementations of universal quantum photonic chips
> generally rely on architectures consisting of localised interactions between the modes that can
> be programmed with known algorithms to implement specific operations. Recent advances have shown
> that interferometers based on 3D continuously coupled waveguide arrays may allow to
> significantly scale up the number of modes with reduced overhead. However, for this class of
> devices, optimal strategies to set the controllable parameters and obtain the desired
> transformation have not been demonstrated generally. In this work, we report the use of a
> machine learning algorithm driven by quantum data to program the operation of a reconfigurable
> three-dimensional continuously coupled photonic device comprising 128 modes. We show
> experimentally the capability of reaching a target unitary matrix, among those achievable by the
> device, through a limited number of one- and two-photon measurements. Our results demonstrate
> that this approach results to be effective in the presence of noise and with a limited amount of
> resources, also allowing to partially compensate imperfections in the experimental setup. This
> method can thus represent a viable route towards programming large scale integrated photonic
> devices, unlocking the possibility of controlling photonic systems for which an analytical
> decomposition is not trivial.
