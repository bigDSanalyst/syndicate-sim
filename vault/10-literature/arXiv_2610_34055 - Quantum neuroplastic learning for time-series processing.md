# Quantum neuroplastic learning for time-series processing

## Abstract

> Quantum machine learning uses quantum states and dynamics to represent and process data. In
> quantum reservoir computing, the physical system is typically fixed and a classical readout is
> trained. Inspired by the formation, strengthening and pruning of synapses during biological
> learning, we propose quantum neuroplastic learning (QNL), which reorganizes the couplings among
> the qubits of a quantum reservoir, much as experience rewires connections among neurons.
> Eligibility traces guide local edits to couplings, fields, gate angles and evolution time, and
> an edit is retained when a statistical bound on its improvement exceeds a complexity cost. We
> evaluate QNL under restarting and online monitored protocols on short- and long-term memory,
> nonlinear autoregressive processing and forecasting, and channel equalization tasks. In
> simulation, QNL enhances memory capacity, develops long-term recall and sustains adaptation
> across multiple tasks, with the largest benefit for tasks that require extended memory. On the
> IBM superconducting processor \texttt{ibm\_fez}, with six seven-qubit reservoirs running in
> parallel on 42 qubits (1.28 million shots in total), configurations learned in simulation and
> frozen before execution reduced the channel-equalization symbol error rate from 9.7\% to 2.2\%
> and achieved a lower error rate than a matched-budget tuned baseline.
