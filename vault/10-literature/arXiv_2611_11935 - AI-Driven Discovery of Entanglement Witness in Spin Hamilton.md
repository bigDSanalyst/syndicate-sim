# AI-Driven Discovery of Entanglement Witness in Spin Hamiltonians

## Abstract

> Entanglement is a crucial resource in quantum science and engineering, but detecting it in many-
> body states from experimentally accessible observables remains challenging. Here we introduce
> EntNet, an AI-driven framework to discover compact combinations of spin observables that
> accurately distinguish entangled from fully separable many-body mixed states in one-dimensional
> spin-$1/2$ chains governed by short-range Hamiltonians. We construct local-unitary-invariant
> representations of measurable Pauli correlators up to four-body order, sparsely select 16
> candidate invariant feature channels, and learn their spatial structure using a Transformer
> architecture with rotary positional embeddings. Using only expectation values of these low-order
> observables, EntNet achieves $>98\%$ recall on certified entangled states with near-zero false-
> positive rates on separable states. Remarkably, it exhibits transferability across system sizes
> and distinct Hamiltonian families, including frustrated and disordered spin models, with minimal
> loss of accuracy. Beyond detection, EntNet provides physical insight into entanglement detection
> by revealing a selective spatial organization of pair correlations, while its finite-temperature
> decision boundary tracks the excitation gap of Jordan-Wigner quasiparticles. These results
> establish a transferable and physically interpretable framework for data-driven discovery of
> entanglement witnesses from local correlation functions.
