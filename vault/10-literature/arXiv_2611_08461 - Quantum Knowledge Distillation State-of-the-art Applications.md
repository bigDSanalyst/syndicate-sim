# Quantum Knowledge Distillation: State-of-the-art, Applications and Open Problems

## Abstract

> Knowledge distillation transfers predictions, representations, or other supervisory information
> between learning models and is increasingly investigated in quantum machine learning. Its
> applications extend beyond compression to cross-architecture transfer, circuit approximation,
> distributed learning, and classical deployment of models trained using quantum-derived
> supervision. This survey reviews these approaches using two complementary classification axes:
> teacher-student configuration and the role of quantum resources in the distillation process.
> Quantum-inspired methods are discussed separately from methods that simulate quantum
> computations classically or execute them on quantum hardware. We explain how measurement
> probabilities, expectation values, classical readouts, state overlaps, and circuit-level
> objectives support different transfer mechanisms. We then critically assess the presented
> results against baseline design, execution conditions, uncertainty, and resource costs. The
> evidence shows that distillation can improve selected quantum students, but benefits are task-
> and architecture-dependent, and several studies find negligible gains or even performance
> degradation. Evaluations on physical quantum hardware remain limited, with some testing
> individual quantum components rather than complete learning pipelines. Heterogeneous protocols
> and incomplete resource reporting prevent general conclusions about practical quantum advantage.
> We identify priorities for controlled benchmarking, noise-aware learning, reproducible
> evaluation, and efficient transfer between quantum and classical models.
