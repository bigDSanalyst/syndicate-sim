# Barren Plateaus in Quantum Linear Combinations: Interference and Lie-Algebraic Control

## Abstract

> A coherent sum of quantum circuits introduces interference that can alter the training signal.
> We distinguish a single linear combination of unitaries (LCU) from a circuit that repeatedly
> alternates branch operations and ancilla mixing. For a single-pass LCU, independent branches
> with zero matrix means cannot erase the differentiated branch's gradient variance: the total
> variance is at least the branch variance times the fourth power of its coefficient magnitude.
> This bound holds at any gate position and does not require the circuit unitaries to be Haar-
> random. Beyond this lower bound, we derive exact loss and gradient formulas that quantify the
> additional contributions from interference between branches. One of these terms depends on
> relative coefficient phases and vanishes when the branch ensemble is invariant under
> multiplication by $i \mathbb{I}$; a four-qubit matchgate example shows that it can be nonzero.
> For repeated interleaving with full special-unitary ancilla control, we determine the generated
> Lie algebra from the associative algebra of the branches, including the available central phase
> directions. This control result predicts concentration only with additional assumptions on
> circuit sampling. Matchgate examples exhibit polynomial single-pass LCU variances and
> exponential concentration under joint Haar-random sampling. We also derive coefficient-selection
> rules and bounds for postselection, measurement error, and approximate moments.
