# Quantum-enhanced mechanical sensing with machine-learning estimation

## Abstract

> Massive mechanical oscillators couple directly to forces, accelerations and gravity, making them
> attractive probes of new physics and of quantum mechanics at macroscopic scales. Pushing
> sensitivities beyond the standard quantum limit for signals of unknown phase requires non-
> Gaussian quantum states, whose metrological advantage has so far been confined to lighter
> systems such as trapped ions. Here we operate a microgram-scale acoustic mode of a high-overtone
> bulk acoustic wave resonator as a phase-insensitive quantum-enhanced sensor. A transmon qubit
> prepares the mode in Fock states of up to seven phonons by optimal control, and resolves its
> phonon-number distribution after interaction with a weak resonant signal. Bayesian analysis
> yields a Fisher information five times the vacuum value, a 7.0 dB gain in signal-amplitude
> sensitivity over any classical probe state, irrespective of its energy, currently limited by
> state preparation infidelities. We then train a neural network on the measured sensor response
> to perform Bayesian estimation without any analytical model of the system, showing the same
> sensitivity gain with half the systematic bias. Our results establish acoustic resonators
> prepared in non-Gaussian states as quantum-enhanced probes for high-frequency gravitational
> waves, dark matter and wave-function collapse.
