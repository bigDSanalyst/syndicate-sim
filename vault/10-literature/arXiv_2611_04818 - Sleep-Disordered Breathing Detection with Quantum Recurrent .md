# Sleep-Disordered Breathing Detection with Quantum Recurrent Neural Networks

## Abstract

> This work investigates the application of Quantum Recurrent Neural Networks to the binary
> classification of sleep-disordered breathing events in time-series data, aiming to distinguish
> between apneic and normal breathing episodes. A variational classical--quantum architecture is
> proposed and evaluated against classical Recurrent Neural Network models. Experiments were
> conducted on multiple real-world datasets with varying levels of complexity to assess the
> strengths and limitations of the quantum approach. In the comparative analysis, both classical
> and quantum models showed comparable performance. The quantum model achieved superior
> performance on the smallest dataset, while yielding slightly weaker results in the most complex
> setting. However, the quantum approach exhibited notable strengths, such as competitive
> performance despite a drastically reduced parameter count and low-qubit regime, as well as good
> generalization capabilities. These results suggest that, despite current hardware and
> algorithmic limitations, variational quantum recurrent models represent a promising alternative
> for time-series classification as Quantum Computing technologies continue to mature.
