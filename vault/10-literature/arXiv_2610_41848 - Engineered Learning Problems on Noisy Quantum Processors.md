# Engineered Learning Problems on Noisy Quantum Processors

## Abstract

> Despite quantum machine learning being an active topic of research, demonstrations of algorithms
> run on quantum hardware beating classical methods remain rare. In this work, we demonstrate a
> significant performance gap using a projected quantum kernel for $n=15,30$ and $45$ qubits over
> classical methods on an engineered classification problem, and show reproducibility of the
> results over different quantum processor units. We also demonstrate how error mitigation can
> widen this gap, both by improving hardware performance but also by increasing the classical
> difficulty, with different techniques contributing unequally to these phenomena. This work
> provides strong evidence that superior empirical performance of quantum hardware over all
> classical techniques is an achievable goal in quantum machine learning, albeit for an artificial
> problem. However, as with any application relying on observables, beating state-of-the-art
> simulation methods remains the primary challenge, as our matrix product state results here show.
