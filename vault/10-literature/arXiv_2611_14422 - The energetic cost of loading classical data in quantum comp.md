# The energetic cost of loading classical data in quantum computers

## Abstract

> Quantum algorithms for machine learning, chemistry, and cryptanalysis often assume coherent
> access to classical data at negligible physical cost. We instead treat data loading as a
> hardware process and quantify the dynamic energetic cost of a quantum random access memory
> (QRAM) query on a superconducting processor. Using the metric-noise-resource framework, we
> relate query fidelity and power to gate duration, relative two-qubit drive strength, and the
> distribution of attenuation across a multi-stage refrigerator, while accounting for thermal
> photons and gate errors. We analyze the linear-depth multiplexer and the noise-resilient bucket-
> brigade architectures, adapting the latter to a native superconducting gate set and refining its
> fidelity analysis using the fully decomposed gate schedule. For a representative bucket-brigade
> QRAM query with a $12$-qubit address register we obtain a query fidelity of $0.969$. The query
> consumes $3.51\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}$ of dynamic energy, corresponding to an average dynamic
> power of $4.38\,\mathrm{W}$ and a peak of $64.4\,\mathrm{W}$. Our optimization shows that, at
> fixed target fidelity, the operating point and attenuation profile can change the average power
> by a factor of two or more.
