# Estimation of Quantum Umlaut Information

## Abstract

> Quantifying correlations is important for understanding how quantum states and channels support
> information processing. Quantum umlaut information $U$ connects such correlations to reliability
> in hypothesis testing and certain communication settings, but its known formulas require
> classical descriptions of the underlying states. To estimate correlations without these
> descriptions, we introduce measured quantum umlaut information $U^M$ by replacing Umegaki
> relative entropy with measured relative entropy. Its variational formulation leads to a quantum
> generative adversarial framework for estimating $U^M$ from measurement statistics of unknown
> bipartite states and classical-quantum channels. This framework optimizes reference states and
> measurements without reconstructing the target states or channel outputs. For classical-quantum
> channels, it also optimizes the input probabilities. We further analyze the relation between
> $U^M$ and $U$, deriving a state-dependent gap bound and characterizing equality for full-rank
> bipartite states. Numerical simulations show trained estimates close on average to independently
> computed numerical references for $U^M$ across the tested states and channels. These estimates
> could help assess how well quantum memories preserve correlations and how noise or drift affects
> input-output dependence in quantum transmitters and state-preparation devices.
