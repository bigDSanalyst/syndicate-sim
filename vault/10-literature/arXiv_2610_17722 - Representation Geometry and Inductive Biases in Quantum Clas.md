# Representation Geometry and Inductive Biases in Quantum Classifiers across Classical Visual Benchmarks

## Abstract

> Quantum Machine Learning (QML) has a theoretical advantage over classical algorithms for
> specific synthetic data that is computationally hard. However, it remains unclear whether such
> theoretical advantages play a meaningful role for realistic classical data, such as natural
> images. Quantum structural properties induced by unitary operations or entanglement introduce
> inductive biases that differ from those of classical models, potentially leading to distinct
> learning behavior on real-world data. In a fully quantum learning pipeline, classical real-world
> data are first encoded into quantum states via a quantum feature mapping, analogous to a
> classical feature extractor, and a quantum classifier then classifies the resulting states.
> Quantum feature mappings generate representations that are difficult to realize classically,
> while also inducing representation-level biases that do not necessarily match the structure of
> realistic data. To enable a fair comparison at the classifier level, we focus on the classifier
> and use classical feature extractors that already provide powerful representations for natural
> images. By controlling the data scale and supervision strength, we analyze how the unitary
> structure of quantum classifiers affects generalization, and show that their effectiveness
> depends strongly on the geometry of the input representations. These results indicate that
> quantum classifiers benefit from classical features that are aligned with quantum feature
> spaces. Our analysis shows that kernel alignment identifies representations compatible with
> quantum feature spaces, providing insights into translating classical models into quantum
> algorithms.
