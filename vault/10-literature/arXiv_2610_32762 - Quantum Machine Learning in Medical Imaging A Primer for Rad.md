# Quantum Machine Learning in Medical Imaging: A Primer for Radiologists

## Abstract

> Deep learning has transformed radiology, but the computational cost of training and deploying
> modern models is outpacing what classical hardware can support. Quantum computing has been
> proposed as one response to this bottleneck: an n-qubit system can represent $2^n$ states
> simultaneously, although practical speedups require efficient algorithms, data encoding, and
> measurement strategies rather than superposition alone. This review introduces radiologists to
> five core quantum concepts, qubits, superposition, entanglement, quantum gates, and quantum
> circuits, and to the superconducting, trapped-ion, and photonic hardware platforms that define
> the current noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era. We then summarize the quantum machine
> learning architectures most often applied to medical imaging (quantum support vector machines,
> quantum neural networks, variational quantum circuits, and quantum convolutional neural
> networks) and quantum image representations such as the flexible representation of quantum
> images (FRQI) and the novel enhanced quantum representation (NEQR). Reported applications
> include brain tumor classification and segmentation on MRI, pneumonia detection on chest
> radiographs, skin and breast lesion classification, CT and PET reconstruction, and ultrasound
> denoising. Nearly all published systems are hybrid quantum-classical pipelines evaluated on
> small, downsampled benchmark datasets or simulators, and clinical deployment is not yet
> feasible. We conclude with current limitations and translational priorities for radiology.
