# Empirical De-Quantization and Norm-Controlled Spectral Audit of Quantum Cognition Machine Learning in an Emerging Equity Market

## Abstract

> Quantum-inspired machine learning in econophysics describes states of financial markets through
> matrix Hamiltonians, using ground-state expectations to predict returns and spectral gaps to
> identify crises. Although early work reports encouraging advances, it remains unclear whether
> these stem from genuine noncommutative quantum geometry or from conventional classical
> regularization and scale effects. We test this in a controlled audit of matrix-form Quantum
> Cognition Machine Learning (QCML) and variational quantum circuits on the Moscow Exchange
> (2020-2025), combining daily equity data with causally lagged macro news under realistic trading
> costs, short-selling bans, and abrupt shifts in market regimes. By using capacity-matched
> operator swaps -- which progressively eliminate complex Berry phases and matrix noncommutativity
> down to a commuting classical Gibbs radial-basis limit under scale-invariant regularization --
> as well as testing spectral indicators against radial-norm null hypotheses and untrained GUE
> random matrices, we isolate the contribution of each quantum element. In out-of-sample data,
> neither complex phases, noncommutativity, nor circuit entanglement improves the ranking of
> returns relative to classical controls. Once feature magnitudes are normalized, apparent crisis
> detection from raw spectral gaps drops below chance levels. These results show that on noisy
> equity panels, matrix-form QCML acts as an adaptive spectrally bounded classical smoothing
> kernel: it reliably integrates lagged news and curbs extreme crisis forecasts without passing
> economic multiple-testing thresholds, while our audit provides operator-swap and random-matrix
> controls for evaluating future quantum-inspired financial models.
