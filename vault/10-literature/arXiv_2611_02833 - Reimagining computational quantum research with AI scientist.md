# Reimagining computational quantum research with AI scientists

## Abstract

> AI scientists are expanding automated research across scientific domains, yet their ability to
> turn the scientific record into a foundation for autonomous discovery remains elusive. Here we
> uncover a fundamental barrier to this transition and demonstrate a route to overcome it in
> computational quantum science, where advances shape both what we can learn from complex quantum
> systems and what quantum computing can ultimately deliver. To identify this barrier, we
> introduce OmniQ, an expert-audited virtual research environment that converts published studies
> into executable reproduction tasks. Across 59 papers spanning classical simulation, quantum
> algorithms and quantum-property characterization, we systematically evaluate five agentic
> systems powered by large language models and six third-party agent harnesses. Our findings
> reveal that current AI scientists often fail to reproduce published results, exposing a
> fundamental mismatch between what scientific papers communicate and what autonomous agents
> require. By contrast, human expert intervention can turn these failures into successful
> reproductions, showing that the missing information can be identified and converted into
> reusable research experience. Guided by this observation, we develop Residual Agents (RA), a
> quantum-tailored self-improving AI scientist that distils externally verified workflow repairs
> into such experience. RA substantially improves research performance under fixed base models and
> limited adaptation budgets. For example, RA raises DeepSeek V4.1 Flash performance from 68% to
> 85%, matching Claude Code with Opus 5 at a third of its cost despite using a weaker base model.
> We integrate all reproduced results into an extensible platform built on the model context
> protocol, making verified research workflows directly accessible to AI scientists and allowing
> new scientific papers to be continuously incorporated.
