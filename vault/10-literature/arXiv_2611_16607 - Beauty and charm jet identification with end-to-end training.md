# Beauty and charm jet identification with end-to-end training on a superconducting quantum processor

## Abstract

> The identification of heavy-flavor jets, i.e. collimated streams of particles originating from
> the fragmentation of beauty and charm quarks produced in proton-proton collisions, represents
> one of the most challenging classification tasks in measurements at Large Hadron Collider
> experiments. In recent years, advanced machine learning techniques have been successfully
> employed to address this problem, while quantum machine learning has emerged as a promising
> alternative approach. However, the high dimensionality of the problem, typically involving
> hundreds of observables, raises an open question: whether the current generation of quantum
> computers, characterized by limited qubit count, can effectively tackle such complex tasks. In
> this work, a variational quantum circuit for the classification of b- and c-jets at the LHCb
> experiment is implemented on a 5-qubit superconducting quantum computer manufactured by IQM and
> installed at the Politecnico di Torino. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first
> demonstration of a quantum algorithm for jet identification using simulated data from an LHC
> experiment in which all stages of the machine-learning workflow, including training and testing,
> are performed on real quantum hardware. The results demonstrate that the impact of hardware-
> induced quantum noise does not significantly degrade the performance. A framework for the
> evaluation of the training time on the quantum hardware and for the circuit optimization is also
> proposed. These findings pave the way for future applications on larger quantum devices based on
> superconducting qubits, featuring a larger number of qubits enabling the encoding of information
> beyond the reach of classical simulation and providing a step toward investigating whether
> quantum advantage may eventually be achievable in high-energy physics classification tasks.
