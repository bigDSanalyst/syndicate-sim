# Quantum resources constrain function approximation

## Abstract

> Universal function approximation is central to machine learning. Quantum learning models are
> universal as well, but this does not specify the resources needed to approximate a given
> function. We study how coherence, imaginarity, entanglement and magic constrain function
> approximation for a fixed encoding and readout, using the cumulative resource power of the
> trainable gates. In a one-qubit model with phase encoding, coherence bounds the oscillation of
> the model function, while imaginarity bounds its odd part. In a two-qubit model, entanglement
> bounds the distance from product functions. These bounds hold at any encoding depth. For magic,
> the phase encoding itself supplies non-Clifford operations. On domains bounded away from zero,
> Clifford trainable gates suffice for uniform approximation. Replacing unrestricted trainable
> gates by Clifford gates increases the encoding depth at most polynomially in the original depth,
> the inverse error and the inverse distance of the domain from zero. Magic in the trainable gates
> can nevertheless improve accuracy at fixed depth. These results show how the resources of a
> quantum model must match the structure of the target function.
